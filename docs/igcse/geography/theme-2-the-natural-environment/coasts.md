# **🌊 CIE IGCSE Geography: Coasts (Comprehensive Notes)**

# **1\. 🌍 Coastal Processes**

## **🔹 Key Definition**

* **Coast**: The area where land meets the sea.  
* **Wave**: Movement of energy through water, usually caused by wind.

## **🔹 Types of Waves**

| Type | Characteristics | Effect |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Constructive waves** | Low energy, long wavelength, strong swash, weak backwash | Build up beaches (deposition) |
| **Destructive waves** | High energy, short wavelength, weak swash, strong backwash | Erode coastlines |

## **🔹 Marine Erosion Processes**

1. **Hydraulic action**  
   * Waves compress air in cracks → rock breaks apart  
2. **Abrasion (corrasion)**  
   * Rocks carried by waves scrape the coastline  
3. **Attrition**  
   * Rocks collide → become smaller and smoother  
4. **Solution (corrosion)**  
   * Chemical dissolution of rocks (e.g. limestone)

## **🔹 Transportation Processes**

1. **Longshore Drift**  
   * Movement of sediment along the coast  
   * Caused by waves approaching at an angle  
   * **Swash** moves material up the beach at an angle  
   * **Backwash** moves it straight down  
2. Other processes:  
   * **Traction** (rolling)  
   * **Saltation** (bouncing)  
   * **Suspension** (floating)  
   * **Solution** (dissolved)

## **🔹 Deposition**

Occurs when:

* Waves lose energy  
* Sediment is dropped

Conditions:

* Shallow water  
* Sheltered areas (e.g. bays)

# **2\. 🌪️ Wind Processes (Coastal Sand Dunes)**

## **🔹 Processes**

* **Deflation**: Wind removes loose sand  
* **Saltation**: Sand grains bounce along surface  
* **Surface creep**: Larger particles roll

# **3\. 🏞️ Coastal Landforms (Erosion)**

## **🔹 Headlands and Bays**

### **Formation:**

* Alternating **hard and soft rock**  
* Soft rock erodes faster → bays  
* Hard rock remains → headlands

### **Features:**

* **Bays**: Calm water, deposition, beaches form  
* **Headlands**: Strong wave attack, erosion dominates

## **🔹 Cliff and Wave-cut Platform**

### **Formation:**

1. Waves erode base of cliff → **wave-cut notch**  
2. Cliff collapses  
3. Process repeats → cliff retreats  
4. Flat area left → **wave-cut platform**

## **🔹 Cave → Arch → Stack → Stump**

### **Formation Sequence:**

1. Crack enlarged → **cave**  
2. Cave deepens → **arch**  
3. Arch collapses → **stack**  
4. Stack erodes → **stump**

👉 Classic example: Old Harry Rocks

# **4\. 🏝️ Coastal Landforms (Deposition)**

## **🔹 Beaches**

* Formed by deposition of sand/shingle  
* Found in **bays (low energy)**

### **Features:**

* **Berm**: Raised ridge  
* **Storm beach**: Larger pebbles at back

**🔹 Spits**

### **Formation:**

* Longshore drift moves sediment  
* Coastline changes direction  
* Sediment deposited into sea → narrow ridge

### **Features:**

* Often curved end (hook)  
* Salt marsh may form behind

👉 Example: Spurn Head

## **🔹 Bars**

* Spit joins two headlands  
* Creates a lagoon behind

## **🔹 Coastal Sand Dunes**

### **Formation:**

1. Sand blown inland from beach  
2. Trapped by obstacles (rocks, vegetation)  
3. Stabilised by plants (e.g. marram grass)

### **Conditions:**

* Strong onshore winds  
* Dry sand supply  
* Vegetation

# **5\. 🌿 Coral Reefs & Mangrove Swamps**

## **🔹 Coral Reefs**

### **Conditions for Growth:**

* Warm water (20–30°C)  
* Shallow water (≤50 m)  
* Clear, unpolluted water  
* Sunlight

### **Types:**

* Fringing reefs  
* Barrier reefs  
* Atolls

👉 Example: Great Barrier Reef

## **🔹 Mangrove Swamps**

### **Conditions:**

* Tropical climates  
* Sheltered coastlines  
* Muddy, low-energy environments

### **Importance:**

* Protect coasts from erosion  
* Nursery for fish  
* Biodiversity

👉 Example: Sundarbans

# **6\. ⚠️ Coastal Hazards**

## **🔹 Coastal Erosion**

* Loss of land due to wave action  
* Threatens homes, infrastructure

## **🔹 Tropical Storms**

* Strong winds \+ heavy rainfall  
* Storm surges cause flooding

👉 Example: Typhoon Haiyan

## **🔹 Other Hazards**

* Cliff collapse  
* Flooding  
* Sea-level rise (climate change)

# **7\. 🌍 Opportunities at the Coast**

## **🔹 Economic Opportunities**

* Tourism (hotels, beaches)  
* Fishing  
* Ports and trade  
* Renewable energy (wave/wind)

## **🔹 Environmental Opportunities**

* Biodiversity (coral reefs, mangroves)  
* Conservation areas

# **8\. 🛠️ Coastal Management**

## **🔹 Hard Engineering**

| Method | Description | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Sea wall** | Concrete barrier | Strong protection | Expensive, ugly |
| **Groynes** | Trap sediment | Builds beaches | Starves other areas |
| **Rock armour** | Large rocks | Absorb wave energy | Costly |

## **🔹 Soft Engineering**

| Method | Description | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Beach nourishment** | Adding sand | Natural look | Needs repeating |
| **Dune stabilisation** | Planting vegetation | Sustainable | Time needed |
| **Managed retreat** | Allow flooding | Cheap, natural | Loss of land |

# **9\. 📍 Case Study Example (Coastal Management)**

## **Holderness Coast, UK**

👉 Holderness Coast

### **Opportunities:**

* Farming (fertile land)  
* Tourism (coastal resorts)

### **Hazards:**

* Rapid erosion (soft clay cliffs)  
* Loss of land and property

### **Management:**

* Sea walls (e.g. at towns)  
* Groynes
